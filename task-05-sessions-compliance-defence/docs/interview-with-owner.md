# docs/interview-with-owner.md

**Interviewee:** The Founder  
**Business:** Genomac Institute  
**Date:** November 13, 2026  

## The Interview

**1. What do customers ask you repeatedly that only you can answer?**
"Learners constantly ask how to bridge the gap between theoretical biology and actually running a bioinformatics workflow. At Genomac Institute, we are focused on equipping our students with hands-on industry skills rather than traditional academic theory, they specifically come to me asking how to set up their local environments, which computing resources to rent, and how to troubleshoot pipeline errors that aren't documented in standard textbooks."

**2. What records do you keep, and where do they live now?**
"We keep track of learner registrations for each of our programs and payment receipts. Assignments are given sometimes, but they are not strictly tracked. Payments are manually tracked through our bank account statements or through other payment providers such as Flutterwave. Student details live in Google Sheets for each program. The operations are largely manual for now."

**3. Has a customer ever asked you to delete their information, and what did you do?**
"Not really, our users usually just unsubscribe from our mailing list if they joined it after signing up for any of our programs through our website."

**4. What would you do if your records were lost tomorrow?**
"It would be an unimaginable nightmare. Although since our users usually sign up for our programs through our website, their details are usually stored in our database before they are added to their respective project groups".

**5. What would make you not use software like this?**
"If it requires a steep learning curve just to upload a new bioinformatics case study, I won't use it. We are researchers and educators, not platform administrators."


## What I Got Wrong About My Own Business Brief

I assumed the platform needed rigorous progress tracking and complex legal data deletion protocols. However, the interview revealed that learner assignments are not currently strictly tracked, and users simply unsubscribe rather than requesting formal account purges. I over-engineered the 30-day grace period deletion flow and progress instrumentation, missing that their actual priority—and primary dealbreaker—is just having a dead-simple, frictionless interface for authors to upload case studies without needing to be platform administrators.
