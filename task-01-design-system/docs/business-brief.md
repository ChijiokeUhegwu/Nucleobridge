# NucleoBridge: Business Brief

## 1. The business

**NucleoBridge** is a browser-based, interactive bioinformatics learning platform that helps learners develop practical skills through guided, real-world case studies. It combines hands-on coding, automated assessment, structured hints, and an AI mentor that guides learners without giving away final answers. Its freemium business model offers free learning steps and a paid PRO subscription. The goal is to make practical bioinformatics training accessible without requiring complex software installations or constant instructor support.

## 2. The person who uses it

The primary user is a **bioinformatics beginner or early-career learner, approximately 18–30 years old**, such as a microbiology student, postgraduate student, or aspiring researcher in Nigeria and similar resource-constrained settings.

They typically use a modest laptop, potentially with 4 GB of RAM, and may rely on mobile hotspots, limited data bundles, and unstable electricity. They have basic computer literacy and some scientific knowledge but may lack command-line experience, programming confidence, or technical support. They need to practise Python, R, and bioinformatics tools without spending hours configuring their environment.

The age range and broader geographic profile are target-user assumptions to validate, not confirmed research findings.

## 3. The one thing that must work

A learner must be able to open a case study, run the required analysis in their browser, check their work, understand why it failed, and resume without losing progress.

The first practical exercise should run quickly on a modest laptop. Feedback must be immediate and trustworthy, with strict automated checks determining pass or fail. Progress should survive interruptions, and paid access should unlock automatically after confirmed payment.

## 4. Three questions NucleoBridge must answer

1. Why did my `script.sh` fail even though I included all the necessary details?
2. Why did my sequence alignment code fail the automated check at stage three?
3. Which specific dataset do I need to load for my current outbreak case?

Answers must come from the academy's authored case studies, error patterns, and tool documentation, informed by the learner's current work. When the platform lacks a reliable answer, it must say so rather than invent one.

## 5. How the interface should feel

The interface should feel **clear, practical, calm, and encouraging**, like a patient technical mentor working beside the learner. It should prioritise the instructions, code editor or terminal, output, and prominent “Check my work” action.

Use plain English, visible progress, actionable error messages, and progressive hints. Keep navigation predictable and the interface lightweight, responsive, and data-conscious. Autosave and recovery should reduce anxiety about power or connection loss. Gamification should make progress rewarding without distracting from learning. The overall experience must help beginners feel capable of solving real bioinformatics problems independently.
